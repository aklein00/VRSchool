# Evidence and citations for the teen game market review

Research cutoff October 29, 2026. Prepared as the evidence appendix to the [product market review](teen-game-market-review.md).

## How to read the evidence

There is no single public dataset establishing the 20 most popular games among exactly 13–17 year olds across all platforms. This review combines youth surveys with current Roblox statistics and mobile market estimates. The resulting set is a product research shortlist, not a unified popularity ranking. No title level statistic is presented as a measured US 13–17 audience share unless the source actually measures that population; none of the sources below provides that complete breakdown.

**Survey preference, active users, concurrent players, lifetime visits, downloads, and revenue answer different questions.** Visits include repeat entries; download counts do not measure retention; store install bands are cumulative; revenue does not identify player age or educational value. Roblox is a platform: its survey share and mobile download rank cannot be assigned to every experience within it.

## S01 JIM youth survey

**Publisher:** Medienpädagogischer Forschungsverbund Südwest. **Source:** [JIM Studie 2025](https://mpfs.de/app/uploads/2025/11/JIM_2025_PDF_barrierearm.pdf). Published 2025; accessed October 29, 2026.

**Population and method:** German youth ages 12–19; total survey n=1,200. The favorite games chart on printed page 54, PDF page 56, labels its base as digital game users n=1,135. Respondents could name up to three favorites without a supplied answer list.

**Claims used:** Minecraft 24%, Fortnite 14%, Roblox 12%, Block Blast 9%, Brawl Stars 8%, Clash Royale 8%, Hay Day 7%. The accompanying discussion is on printed page 53. These are mentions among favorites, not exclusive market shares.

**Limitation:** Older than the 2026 evidence; Germany and ages 12–19 do not equal US ages 13–17. The published chart base is used rather than the broader denominator stated in some secondary summaries.

## S02 Norwegian youth research in 2026

**Publisher:** Norwegian Media Authority, Medietilsynet. **Source:** [Barn og medier 2026](https://www.medietilsynet.no/fakta/rapporter/barn-og-medier/2026/barn-og-medier-26), updated August 10, 2026; accessed October 29, 2026. Relevant sections 6.1 and 6.3, figures 33 and 34.

**Method and claims used:** Ages 9–18 could name three games they play most; 1,397 supplied at least one game. Roblox, Fortnite, Minecraft, FC/FIFA, and Clash Royale lead the overall responses. Hay Day is identified among teenage girls' favorites. The report says Brawl Stars fell out of its youngest boys' top five in 2026, illustrating regional change.

**Limitation:** Mixed age groups and Norway; responses include both platforms and individual Roblox experiences. Some popular Roblox titles skew younger in this study. It cannot establish each experience's teen share.

## S03 North American Gen Z context

**Publisher:** YPulse Editorial Team. **Source:** [Gen Zs Top 10 Favorite Video and Mobile Games in 2026](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2026/05/07/gen-zs-top-10-favorite-video-and-mobile-games-in-2026/), May 7, 2026; accessed October 29, 2026.

**Claim used:** The public summary names Fortnite and Roblox among leading Gen Z favorites. It also identifies Subway Surfers as a new name on that year's list.

**Limitation:** Gen Z includes adults. The complete ranking, sample detail, and age cuts are not available in the public article, so the review does not infer an exact rank or teen percentage from it.

## S04 Mobile market performance in September 2026

**Publisher:** Sensor Tower; author Donny Kristianto. **Source:** [Top 10 Worldwide Mobile Games By Revenue and Downloads in September 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-september-2026), October 2026; accessed October 29, 2026.

**Claims used:** Worldwide mobile game downloads ranked Roblox first, Free Fire second, Block Blast third, and Subway Surfers fourth. Honor of Kings led estimated worldwide mobile revenue. These are September 2026 rankings, not an October final result.

**Coverage:** App Store and Google Play estimates; third party Android markets excluded. The article describes Honor of Kings' strength in China. No age segmentation is supplied.

**Interpretation limit:** Strong commercial or acquisition performance supports inclusion in a competitive set. It does not prove popularity among US teens, retention, or learning outcomes. The review uses the text's rankings and does not estimate dollars from chart images.

## S06 Historical Roblox breakout performance

**Publisher:** Roblox. **Source:** [The 2025 Roblox Replay Decoded Through Search and Style](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/12/roblox-replay-decoded-search-style), December 16, 2025; accessed October 29, 2026.

**Claims used:** Roblox reported Grow a Garden at 21.6 million concurrent users in July 2025, and Steal a Brainrot above 25 million in September 2025.

**Limitation:** Historical event peaks are not normal engagement or current counts. Comparing those peaks with one October 2026 snapshot illustrates why peaks should not be treated as a stable audience size; it does not calculate retention or a decline rate.

## S07 Genshin Impact store evidence

**Publishers:** Google Play and COGNOSPHERE. **Source:** [Genshin Impact official Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.miHoYo.GenshinImpact), accessed October 29, 2026.

**Claims used:** Listing shows 100 million plus cumulative Google Play downloads and a September 9, 2026 update. It describes exploration, elemental reactions, characters, and cooperative play.

**Limitation:** A cumulative install band establishes reach, not current active users or an age specific ranking. This is a durable fantasy product and art reference with a weaker current demand measure than the monthly and concurrent statistics elsewhere.

## S08 EA SPORTS FC Mobile store evidence

**Publishers:** Google Play and Electronic Arts. **Source:** [EA SPORTS FC Soccer Mobile official Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.ea.gp.fifamobile), accessed October 29, 2026.

**Claims used:** 500 million plus cumulative Google Play downloads. The current listing is branded Soccer Mobile 27 and shows a September 18, 2026 update. It describes team building, quick matches, leagues, and tournaments.

**Limitation:** This app has a long operating history and changing seasonal branding. The install band is not downloads of the current season, active users, or evidence that the broader FC/FIFA survey share belongs specifically to mobile.

## S13 UCLA-affiliated fantasy and friendship audience research, 2024

**Source:** Burrus, A., Rivas-Lara, S., Hines, A., & Uhls, Y. T. (2024). [Teens and Screens 2024](https://www.scholarsandstorytellers.com/teens-screens-24). Center for Scholars & Storytellers at UCLA. Reviewed October 29, 2026.

**Population and findings used:** 1,644 US respondents aged 10–24, surveyed August 2024. 36.2% preferred content featuring fantasy worlds; 63.5% wanted more content centered on friendships and platonic relationships.

**Limits:** University-affiliated audience report, not a peer-reviewed journal paper. Results concern a broad age range and stated media preferences, not a US 13–17-only estimate, D&D participation, or learning outcomes. Read together with S14 rather than implying an uninterrupted increase in fantasy preference.

**Creative interpretation:** Test a fantasy world with recognizable relationships, expressive mentors and welcoming school spaces. This is an art-direction proposal, not a causal study finding.

## S14 UCLA-affiliated audience update, 2025

**Source:** Puretz, M., Hines, A., & Uhls, Y. T. (2025). [Get Real! Relatability on Demand. Teens & Screens 2025](https://www.scholarsandstorytellers.com/teens-screens-25). Center for Scholars & Storytellers. Reviewed October 29, 2026.

**Population and findings used:** 1,500 US respondents aged 10–24, surveyed August 2025. Relatable stories moved ahead of fantasy; the report continued to find interest in friendship-centered stories.

**Limits:** University-affiliated audience report, not a journal paper or experimental result. The shift qualifies S13; it is not proof that fantasy has lost all appeal or that a fantasy setting should be abandoned.

**Creative interpretation:** Ground extraordinary characters and environments in recognizable emotions and relationships; validate with actual teen players.

## S15 D&D, young-adult identity and player experiences, 2026

**Source:** Thomas, Z., Dunn, A., Gomez Bergin, A. D., & Hazell, C. M. (2026). [D&D and You: A Reflexive Thematic Analysis of Young Adult Players’ Experiences Exploring Identity and Mental Health Through Dungeons and Dragons](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13295500/). *Behavioral Sciences*, 16(6), 1026. DOI: 10.3390/bs16061026. Published June 18, 2026; reviewed October 29, 2026.

**Method and findings used:** Peer-reviewed qualitative paper using interviews with 11 UK D&D players aged 18–25 and reflexive thematic analysis. Themes: D&D as a safer space, for coping, for exploration and for growth.

**Limits:** Small sample of existing adult players; experiences are self-reported. This is not a measure of teen/Gen Alpha audience size or fantasy-media consumption, and does not establish causal educational or therapeutic effects for VRHS.

**Creative interpretation:** Test room for player identity and belonging through character variety, costumes, gestures and personal spaces. Do not present that proposal as a measured result.

## Attribution of recommendations
