# VRSchool teen game market review

Product strategy brief for players ages 13–17

October 29, 2026

## Visual references to prioritize

**Recommended direction: a fantasy academy with Royale High's sense of place, Brawl Stars's character clarity, and Adopt Me!'s warmth.** Keep the teacher designs original, give students meaningful creative expression, and test the result with both younger and older teens.

The recovered VRSchool material establishes a written setting, not an approved look. Its only Photoshop composite is an AI tools infographic. The matches below are proposals for the friendly monster faculty, hilltop campus, and magical classrooms described in the concept documents. Visual fit is a creative judgment rather than evidence of teen preference. Official visual references are collected in [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references).

## Specialist perspectives on the top choices

The following edited blurbs come from separate game designer and art direction agents. Their complete assessments and prototype ideas are available in the [game designer notes](game-designer-notes.md) and [art direction notes](art-direction-notes.md).

### Royale High

**Game designer:** A campus becomes valuable when it gives students a place to belong and a way to contribute. Connect classroom achievements to visible personal and shared expression. A short orientation could pair one classroom challenge with a crest displayed on a communal noticeboard.

**Art director:** Establish a memorable campus outline and one landmark per department. Differentiate teachers through material families such as ice and frosted stone, warm brass and paper, or botanical glass. Keep the invitation of a magical school while accommodating more than one visual taste.

### Brawl Stars

**Game designer:** Borrow the clarity of a small space and a concrete shared objective. A three person experiment can assign observation, manipulation, and checking roles, then rotate them. Success should require reasoning together and explaining the outcome.

**Art director:** Build each teacher around a distinct silhouette, gesture, and teaching prop visible at phone size. Test the faculty in monochrome before adding detail. Warm expressions and patient movement should make even unusual creatures read as mentors.

### Adopt Me!

**Game designer:** Persistent relationships can make a world feel personally owned. A magical companion could respond to the student's evidence based habitat decisions. Test whether care reinforces understanding rather than encouraging repeated guesses or collection for its own sake.

**Art director:** Rounded shapes and uncomplicated surfaces can make a spider or mimic approachable. Add academic costume, asymmetry, and controlled material contrast so the faculty have the presence of teachers and appeal beyond a very young audience.

### Dress To Impress

**Game designer:** Its useful lesson is a clear creative prompt followed by a public presentation. Apply that structure to art, drama, or language tasks with multiple valid solutions. Use a visible learning rubric and specific peer feedback rather than popularity votes as assessment.

**Art director:** Controlled lighting and presentation make individual choices legible. Create styling and expression systems that work across different bodies and creature proportions. Treat this as an identity reference rather than the default anatomy or architecture for the whole game.

## Product recommendation

VRSchool's strongest opportunity is **a social fantasy place where doing something interesting together produces both mastery and identity**. Start with one memorable teacher and a short cooperative activity. A complete campus, 13 fully functioning AI teachers, and a large open world would obscure the first question: will teens choose to return to a class because the activity itself is enjoyable?

Our initial study priorities are Royale High for context, Dress To Impress for creative presentation, Brawl Stars for objective clarity, Minecraft for construction, and Adopt Me! for attachment. Market leaders in other genres remain valuable for understanding access, progression, and social coordination, even when their aesthetics are a poor fit.

## What the market evidence supports

The strongest directly measured youth preferences in this source set favor Minecraft and Fortnite, with Roblox also prominent. Germany's 2025 JIM survey reports favorite mentions of 24%, 14%, and 12%, respectively. It also identifies Block Blast, Brawl Stars, Clash Royale, and Hay Day. These figures cover German game users ages 12–19 and are not US 13–17 market shares. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey)

Norway's 2026 research also places Roblox, Fortnite, and Minecraft at the front of its youth responses. A North American Gen Z summary from YPulse provides additional directional support for Fortnite and Roblox, but includes adults. These sources support investigating the audience; they do not establish the same ordering in every country or age segment. [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026), [S03](citations.md#s03-north-american-gen-z-context)

The 20 profiles below combine that youth evidence with current Roblox activity, September mobile rankings, and durable commercial reach. **They are a competitive research set, not a statistically comparable top 20 ranking for exactly ages 13–17.** Nine are specific Roblox experiences; eleven are standalone games, all with a mobile version. Roblox itself is not counted again as a game.

The evidence falls into three groups: direct youth preference for Minecraft, Fortnite, Brawl Stars, Clash Royale, Block Blast, and Hay Day; all age engagement for individual Roblox experiences; and broader commercial or reach evidence for Free Fire, Subway Surfers, Honor of Kings, FC Mobile, and Genshin Impact. Genshin and FC Mobile rely partly on cumulative store install bands, so their inclusion carries less evidence of current activity than a recent monthly ranking.

## Twenty games to study

For all Roblox profiles, playing counts refer to one official snapshot on October 29, 2026 at 12:54 PM Pacific. They include all ages. Platform focus means Roblox mobile and desktop; it does not assert headset compatibility. Every strength and VRSchool opportunity below is product analysis, not a measured cause of success.

### 01 Brookhaven RP

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 428,941 playing; 89.01 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** Familiar social spaces let players invent their own roles and stories. The world supplies context while friends supply much of the activity, making the setting useful beyond a fixed mission sequence.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Give students a small, legible campus with clubs and shared spaces between classes. Test whether a player can find a friend, adopt a role, and start an activity without a lengthy tutorial. Open roleplay requires enough structure that the learning activity remains discoverable.

### 02 Murder Mystery 2

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 489,212 playing; 31.93 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** Different hidden roles create uncertainty, observation, and conversation inside a repeatable round. Players can understand the core situation quickly while reading one another remains difficult.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Build a classroom mystery where students reconstruct a failed experiment from evidence distributed among teammates. Retain deduction and discussion while replacing lethal roles with a constructive investigation. Ensure the explanation matters more than guessing whom to accuse.

### 03 Steal a Brainrot

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 256,240 playing; 74.23 billion lifetime visits. Roblox separately reported a historical event peak above 25 million in September 2025. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics), [S06](citations.md#s06-historical-roblox-breakout-performance)

**Strength:** Recognizable absurd characters, visible ownership, collection, and the possibility of loss create immediate social stakes and shareable moments.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Use surprising original faculty, visible discoveries, and voluntary show and tell. Let students exchange knowledge or contribute to a shared exhibit. For a welcoming school, permanent theft of earned work would undermine the intended relationship; test playful competition without that loss.

### 04 Adopt Me!

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 220,810 playing; 45.25 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** Companion care, home decoration, and social routines turn progression into a personal relationship. Friendly forms make a busy social world approachable. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** A student maintained greenhouse or companion habitat can preserve progress between classes. Reward accurate observation with visible environmental improvement. Keep compulsory care small so returning feels rewarding rather than like overdue chores.

### 05 Blox Fruits

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 138,669 playing; 65.14 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** Character builds and a long progression path give players reasons to master systems and discuss their choices. A recognizable adventure structure helps organize a large amount of content.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Replace a grind for power with visible mastery of a few magical tools. Different academic approaches could unlock alternate solutions to the same problem. Give the first useful ability early and make later advancement demonstrate understanding rather than time spent.

### 06 RIVALS

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 153,458 playing; 19.02 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** Direct contests, explicit objectives, and replayable rounds provide clear feedback on improvement. A small match structure makes a repeat attempt easy to understand.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Test a short team challenge with a visible success condition, immediate feedback, and an easy rematch. A physics apparatus or word construction duel could provide the structure. Compare cooperation with competition before adding public rankings that may discourage less experienced students.

### 07 99 Nights in the Forest

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 150,711 playing; 30.31 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot; the Norwegian study identifies especially young audiences for this title, so teen fit needs validation. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics), [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026)

**Strength:** A threatened shared camp gives a group a concrete reason to coordinate tasks and prepare together. Recurring danger produces memorable stories from ordinary resource decisions.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Translate that interdependence into a magical field trip: observe a habitat, gather samples, and stabilize a safe camp before a weather change. Keep the readable preparation loop while tuning tension to the school's welcoming tone.

### 08 Dress To Impress

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 84,030 playing; 11.26 billion lifetime visits. All ages, one snapshot. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics)

**Strength:** A clear theme, constrained making time, and a public reveal make personal choices understandable to other players. Outfits become a shared conversation rather than an isolated customization menu. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Use creative briefs for art and drama classes, followed by a showcase and a short explanation of choices. Separate cosmetic self expression from academic evaluation. A successful result should communicate an idea, not depend on winning a popularity contest.

### 09 Grow a Garden

**Platform focus:** Roblox mobile and desktop. **Evidence:** 12,748 playing; 36.00 billion lifetime visits. Its historical July 2025 peak was 21.6 million concurrent users. Treat it as a major breakout case with a smaller present snapshot, not a current leader inferred from its peak. [S05](citations.md#s05-roblox-public-statistics), [S06](citations.md#s06-historical-roblox-breakout-performance)

**Strength:** Planting, waiting, and harvesting make progress tangible. A visible collection and growth while offline can give returning players a simple next action.

**VRSchool opportunity:** A botanical classroom can preserve student experiments between sessions. Let players predict an outcome, return to inspect it, and explain the result. Schedule changes around learning and convenience rather than making rare time windows the primary reason to return.

### 10 Minecraft

**Platforms:** Mobile, PC, and console. **Evidence:** 24% favorite mentions in the German youth survey; also among Norway's leading youth games in 2026. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey), [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026)

**Strength:** Players build and revise objects within a coherent world. Creation and survival support different motivations, while the same materials can serve many player authored goals. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Let students construct working models, run a test, and revise their explanation. A bridge or ecosystem should visibly respond to their choices. Begin with a small construction vocabulary so touch controls and a crowded tool menu do not become the challenge.

### 11 Fortnite

**Platforms:** PC, console, Android, and iOS, subject to regional and device availability. **Evidence:** 14% favorite mentions in the German youth survey; prominent in both 2026 Norwegian youth research and YPulse's North American Gen Z summary. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey), [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026), [S03](citations.md#s03-north-american-gen-z-context), [S10](citations.md#s10-fortnite-mobile-availability)

**Strength:** A shared identity spans social play, creator experiences, and recurring events. Players can arrive with friends and find several reasons to remain in the same ecosystem.

**VRSchool opportunity:** Keep student identity consistent across different classes. One small school festival could connect creative work and cooperative challenges. Establish a repeatable class format before committing to the content volume expected from a large live service.

### 12 Brawl Stars

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** 8% favorite mentions in the German youth survey. Supercell's 2026 retrospective also reports both setbacks and improving momentum, so fame should not be equated with steady growth everywhere. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey), [S12](citations.md#s12-brawl-stars-operating-lessons)

**Strength:** Distinct characters and concrete team objectives make compact matches legible on phones. Players can learn a role without first understanding a large world. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Design small cooperative rounds with observation, manipulation, and checking roles. Track explanation quality alongside completion. Give each teacher and classroom prop a distinct silhouette so visual recognition supports play instead of adding reading overhead.

### 13 Clash Royale

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** 8% favorite mentions in the German youth survey; among the leading titles in Norway's 2026 youth responses. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey), [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026)

**Strength:** A limited hand of cards supports strategic combinations and readable tradeoffs in a small space. Players plan a loadout, test it, and adjust. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Give students a small deck of investigation tools or argument techniques and ask them to explain their selections. Permit several successful combinations. Keep necessary tools accessible through demonstrated skill rather than paid upgrades.

### 14 Block Blast

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** Number three worldwide by September 2026 mobile downloads; 9% favorite mentions in the German youth survey. [S04](citations.md#s04-mobile-market-performance-in-september-2026), [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey)

**Strength:** Placing blocks and clearing lines provides simple controls, visible consequences, and increasingly demanding planning. It does not require social coordination or a long introduction. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Study how quickly a player can understand a puzzle through interaction. Use an equally legible spatial task in the Yeti's room, but require reasoning that connects to the intended learning objective. Popularity of a puzzle does not establish educational transfer.

### 15 Free Fire

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** Number two worldwide by mobile downloads in September 2026. No teen share is disclosed. [S04](citations.md#s04-mobile-market-performance-in-september-2026)

**Strength:** A phone oriented multiplayer format, short sessions, recognizable characters, and squad play give friends a clear shared activity. Its publisher emphasizes broad smartphone access. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Treat performance and join friction as product requirements. Test the cooperative classroom on modest devices and uneven connections. Borrow quick group formation and understandable objectives; the military setting and gunplay have little direct fit to the current school premise.

### 16 Subway Surfers

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** Number four worldwide by September 2026 mobile downloads; named in YPulse's 2026 Gen Z summary. The market metric refers to the original game, not the separate Subway Surfers City. [S04](citations.md#s04-mobile-market-performance-in-september-2026), [S03](citations.md#s03-north-american-gen-z-context)

**Strength:** An immediate runner loop and changing destinations make a familiar action easy to revisit. Stylized characters and locations give repeated play a changing visual context. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Prototype a brief, readable between class traversal challenge and optional personal bests. Keep meaningful learning in choices and explanations rather than inserting quiz prompts into fast movement. A runner camera would also need separate headset comfort evaluation.

### 17 Hay Day

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** 7% favorite mentions in the German youth survey; Norway's 2026 report also identifies popularity among teenage girls. [S01](citations.md#s01-jim-youth-survey), [S02](citations.md#s02-norwegian-youth-research-in-2026)

**Strength:** A persistent farm, gentle production routines, and social contribution make gradual progress visible. The environment feels personally maintained rather than repeatedly reset. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** A shared greenhouse or workshop could connect several subjects through visible projects. Let a player's contribution remain on campus and be useful to friends. Avoid making repetitive collection the price of entering the activity students actually came to play.

### 18 Honor of Kings

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** Number one worldwide by estimated mobile game revenue in September 2026, with strength in China. This is a commercial benchmark, not proof of a leading US teen audience. [S04](citations.md#s04-mobile-market-performance-in-september-2026)

**Strength:** Clear team roles and heroes with distinct abilities make individual specialization useful to collective success. Its visual language links character identity to function. [S11](citations.md#s11-official-game-and-visual-references)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Give different student tools complementary uses during a group experiment, and rotate roles so everyone practices each skill. Keep the role clarity; a full competitive arena and long mastery curve would be disproportionate to a first classroom prototype.

### 19 EA SPORTS FC Mobile

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. **Evidence:** 500 million plus cumulative Google Play downloads. EA's FY26 release reports growth across its football products including FC Mobile. Broader FC/FIFA youth mentions are not attributed specifically to this mobile title. [S08](citations.md#s08-ea-sports-fc-mobile-store-evidence), [S09](citations.md#s09-ea-financial-context)

**Strength:** Team composition, short competitive formats, and leagues connect individual collection to a social identity. A roster gives players a vocabulary for discussing strengths and combinations. [S08](citations.md#s08-ea-sports-fc-mobile-store-evidence)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Create clubs where students assemble complementary skills and contribute to group projects. Make each contribution visible and useful. The relevant lesson is team identity, not expensive roster acquisition or importing professional sports licenses.

### 20 Genshin Impact

**Platforms:** Mobile, PC, and console. **Evidence:** 100 million plus cumulative Google Play downloads and a September 2026 update. This is evidence of established reach and continued operation; it is a weaker current popularity measure than the monthly and concurrent data above. No exact teen audience is public in this source. [S07](citations.md#s07-genshin-impact-store-evidence)

**Strength:** Distinct characters, elemental interactions, and discoverable landscapes connect emotional attachment with systems exploration. A consistent fantasy presentation makes individual locations feel part of a larger world. [S07](citations.md#s07-genshin-impact-store-evidence)

**VRSchool opportunity:** Reuse a few understandable magical rules across classrooms so a lesson transfers to a new context. Take visual composition and discovery as references while limiting the first build to one room and courtyard. A large character collection and open world are unnecessary to test that hypothesis.

## Decisions this research should drive

1. **Validate social making first.** Build one creative or experimental class with a visible outcome students can share. Compare solo and paired play.
2. **Test a mobile accessible route alongside the VR idea.** Roblox and standalone phone games provide strong audience signals, but these sources do not decide the engine or prove headset demand. Compare access, controls, comfort, and the quality of the learning interaction.
3. **Make success more specific than time spent.** Measure task understanding, unaided completion, explanation quality, voluntary replay, and whether friends improve the experience. Follow up on learning after the initial novelty has passed.
4. **Test visual age fit.** Show original teacher silhouettes and two classroom treatments to groups spanning 13–15 and 16–17. Check warmth, perceived maturity, recognition, and willingness to inhabit the space.
5. **Limit the first scope.** One teacher, one room, one cooperative activity, one lasting reward. Expand the faculty only after the class earns repeat interest.

The market evidence identifies useful places to look. It does not validate VRSchool's demand, its learning effectiveness, or a particular business model. Those are the next product experiments.
