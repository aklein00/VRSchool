# Game designer notes for VRSchool

Prepared October 29, 2026 by the game designer agent. These are design judgments and prototype recommendations for ages 13–17, not measured teen preferences or a popularity ranking. They respond to the existing faculty, classroom, and welcoming campus concepts. Game mechanics are grounded in the official sources linked below; all proposed VRSchool features are recommendations.

## The strongest starting point

Combine the magical school identity of **Royale High**, the expressive making and presentation loop of **Dress To Impress**, and the clarity of **Brawl Stars** objectives. Use **Adopt Me!** to study companionship, **Minecraft** for player authorship, and **Genshin Impact** for environmental discovery. Build one satisfying class before planning a complete school or a large open world.

## Royale High

**Designer perspective.** The closest thematic reference is a magical social world where avatar presentation and a sense of place give players reasons to inhabit a campus. Its current official listing emphasizes seasonal realms, dorm furniture, cosmetic expression, and player writing recognition. For VRSchool, connect academic activities to how students express themselves and contribute to shared spaces; avoid making education feel like a toll paid to reach the social game. The opportunity is a campus identity earned through useful, visible contributions.

**Prototype.** In a ten minute orientation, a student meets one original monster teacher, solves a paired classroom challenge, then earns a personal crest displayed on a shared classroom noticeboard. Test whether players can explain what they learned and want to invite a friend.

**Official reference:** [Royale High by callmehbob](https://www.roblox.com/games/735030788/Royale-High). The reference is to the official experience; similarly named copies are excluded.

## Dress To Impress

**Designer perspective.** Creating a look, presenting it, and seeing other players' interpretations makes individual expression legible to a group. The official listing highlights outfits, runway poses, collaboration, and voting. VRSchool can apply that public making loop to art, drama, and language arts. Give students a clear creative constraint and enough tools to produce different valid answers. Evaluate the intended skill with a visible rubric; popularity voting alone is a weak assessment mechanism.

**Prototype.** Oobleck's workshop asks pairs to build a creature costume that communicates a chosen habitat. Students present the costume and explain two design choices. Peers give one specific compliment; the teacher checks the explanation against the habitat brief.

**Official reference:** [Dress To Impress](https://www.roblox.com/games/15101393044/Dress-To-Impress).

## Adopt Me!

**Designer perspective.** Pets, a customizable home, and friendly social routines make a world feel personally owned. The official site and game listing connect raising pets, decorating, collecting, and roleplay. VRSchool's opportunity is a persistent relationship with a companion that grows as students demonstrate understanding. For teenagers, let that relationship express competence and personality rather than relying only on cute presentation. Make advancement understandable and earned through play.

**Prototype.** A student adopts an original magical greenhouse creature. During a science class, the student diagnoses its needs by observing light, water, and soil clues. Correct explanations alter the habitat and unlock a cosmetic growth stage. Test whether students use evidence rather than guess repeatedly.

**Official references:** [Adopt Me website](https://www.playadopt.me/) and [official Roblox experience](https://www.roblox.com/games/920587237/Adopt-Me).

## Minecraft

**Designer perspective.** Its strongest lesson is the difference between answering a question and constructing something that works. Official Creative mode removes resource constraints, while Survival introduces resource and environmental demands. VRSchool should let students model and revise ideas with an immediate consequence they can observe. Limit the initial toolset so making remains accessible on touchscreens and does not overwhelm the intended learning task.

**Prototype.** Two students build a bridge across the Ettin's map room using a small set of beams and supports. They predict which span will hold a load, run the simulation, and revise the design. Save a before and after model plus their explanation as the reward and learning record.

**Official references:** [What is Minecraft](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/about-minecraft) and [Minecraft game modes](https://help.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/360058743992-Minecraft-Differences-Between-Creative-Survival).

## Genshin Impact

**Designer perspective.** Traversal, discoverable secrets, elemental puzzles, and a party of distinct characters connect exploration to systems learning. Its official game description explicitly links elements to puzzle solving and supports playing with friends. VRSchool can use a small number of understandable magical rules across several classrooms. Reusing those rules in new contexts gives students transfer problems instead of unrelated quiz screens. Treat the scale of its world as a long term reference, not a first prototype scope.

**Prototype.** The Yeti's room contains three ice channels. Students redirect heat and water to restore a broken instrument, predict the next state, then explain the result. One rule learned here reappears in the greenhouse so the second puzzle tests transfer.

**Official reference:** [Genshin Impact game features](https://genshin.hoyoverse.com/m/en/game).

## Brawl Stars

**Designer perspective.** Clear team objectives and a compact play space help friends understand what to do together. Supercell describes quick matches, while its support documentation distinguishes modes by concrete objectives such as collecting gems or occupying zones. VRSchool can borrow that clarity for short cooperative classroom rounds. Reward coordinated reasoning and useful role changes; keep the learning explanation visible after the action ends.

**Prototype.** Three students stabilize a magical experiment: one observes instrument readings, one moves components, and one checks a shared rule sheet. Rotate jobs each round. Score the team's explanation and successful coordination, with an untimed practice version for accessibility.

**Official references:** [Brawl Stars](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/) and [Supercell game modes](https://support.supercell.com/brawl-stars/en/articles/game-modes-13.html).

## Prototype decision

Start with Oobleck's workshop or the Yeti's room. Recruit teens across the full 13–17 range and test task comprehension, completion without help, explanation quality, and willingness to replay with a friend. Compare a solo version with a paired version. These tests should decide whether social making, cooperative problem solving, or exploration becomes the main loop.
