# VRSchool project status

October 29, 2026

VRSchool is at the concept and research stage. The project has a recognizable premise and a draft faculty roster, but no playable prototype, chosen development platform, or approved visual direction.

## What exists

- A welcoming fantasy school premise in which friendly monster teachers use interactive activities, games, and puzzles.
- A roster of 13 faculty roles covering 12 subjects and a headmaster, with creatures, character classes, attributes, locations, and genders.
- A campus and surrounding town concept, plus nine complete classroom descriptions and one partial music section. Drama, technology, and headmaster environments have no dedicated descriptions in the recovered document.
- A single sheet workbook containing the faculty roster.
- One recovered Photoshop file. Its composite is an AI tools infographic, not character or campus artwork. Individual Photoshop layers have not been audited.
- Six empty production folders, preserved as a scaffold.
- A new product market review, separate evidence document, dated Roblox statistics, and specialist game design and art direction notes.

The original local folder and Drive documents are preserved. Local exports have recorded checksums in `data/source-manifest.json`.

## Decisions still open

| Decision | Current state | Suggested next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | The current brief targets ages 13–17; original prose sometimes refers to middle school | Test younger and older teens separately |
| Core promise | Social world, school subjects, and AI assistance are proposed | Decide which player benefit leads the first experience |
| Platform | VR is in the premise; no engine, device, or distribution decision exists | Compare a Roblox desktop and mobile prototype with a headset prototype |
| Main loop | No implemented loop or tested session design | Build one teacher, one room, one cooperative task, and one reward |
| Learning | No curriculum mapping or assessment model | Select one learning objective and define observable evidence of understanding |
| Art direction | Written setting exists; approved concept art does not | Test faculty silhouettes and one classroom scene |
| Faculty identity | Draft uses Dungeons & Dragons inspired names and creature types | Decide the intended identity and develop original designs accordingly |
| AI teachers | Personalized feedback is an aspiration | Validate authored teaching interactions before expanding AI behavior |
| Product model | No business model or operating plan | Establish intended setting of use and who would choose or pay for it |

## Recommended first playable test

Build Oobleck's workshop as a 5–10 minute cooperative creative challenge, or the Yeti's room as a short prediction and experimentation puzzle. Include a brief introduction, one meaningful choice, a visible outcome, and a reason to show the result to a friend.

Keep the campus small enough that players can reach the activity immediately. Measure whether teens understand the objective, finish without intervention, explain what changed, and choose to replay or invite a friend. These are proposed validation criteria, not existing results.

## Draft issues to resolve during development

The environment document contains incomplete sentences and some conflicting character details. For example, the Displacer Beast is female in the faculty roster but uses male pronouns in the location text. Some dangerous or hostile classroom descriptions also conflict with the stated compassionate teaching tone. Preserve the original exports as historical source material and make deliberate revisions once the product direction is chosen.
